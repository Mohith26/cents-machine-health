How it works

Many failures never raise an error code: a pulse washer with a stuck door lock still takes the card tap, a dryer with a dead igniter still tumbles. Customers notice first, and they leave a trail in the turn data Cents already has. Machine Health runs a small hidden Markov model per machine with four states: working, not being used (customers avoid it), takes payment, doesn't start, and for dryers runs without heat. Every open hour it scores what the machine did against what it should have done given how busy that store actually was:

  • Turns vs. expected: the machine's trailing share of its group (days d-15 to d-2, so a failure can't drag its own baseline down) times the store's actual traffic that hour, negative binomial with dispersion fitted from the fleet.
  • Relocations: a card customer starts this machine, then a same-size sibling within 7 minutes.
  • Re-dries: a card customer starts a dryer again 35 to 70 minutes later.
  • Refunds and Cents Assist calls that name the machine, against the machine's turns in the last 4 hours.
